RunningCyclingAnalysis

Privacy policy

Last updated 23 October 2026.

RunningCyclingAnalysis analyses your activity files in your browser. If you sign in, some of your data is stored on Cloudflare so it follows you between devices. This page says what is stored, where, for how long, and how to delete it. We do not sell your data. The app contains no analytics or advertising trackers.

Who operates the service

RunningCyclingAnalysis is operated by Bryce Capuano. The source code is public at GitHub. Privacy questions and requests go to the repository's issues. Do not put personal data, activity files or account details in a public issue. Say that you have a privacy request, and you will be asked for the minimum needed to act on it.

Without an account

Files you analyse without signing in are read and processed in your browser. They are not uploaded, and nothing from them is stored on our servers. So that your results are still there when you come back, the app keeps a summary of each activity in your browser's IndexedDB storage. It is the analysis summary described below for accounts, without the original file and without any file hash: the activity name and sport, the start time, distance, duration and elevation gain, the heart-rate and power figures, the best efforts, climb sections and data-quality flags. Its identifier includes the name of the file it came from. It holds no GPS coordinates and no per-second recording, and it does not expire. The app also saves your body mass, bike mass, unit preference, and your year of birth and sex if you give them, in your browser's local storage, and records there that the first-analysis questions were answered, so they are not asked again. The settings copy in local storage is kept whether or not you have an account. Nothing else is written there.

Start over, offered only when you are signed out, removes every summary kept in this browser, and so does clearing this site's data in your browser. You can also delete one activity from the Kept in this browser list on your Profile page. Signing out does not remove the summaries. Anyone using the same browser profile can see them, so use Start over on a shared computer.

Year of birth and sex are optional. They are used only to grade your results against age standards. Without an account they stay in your browser; with an account they are kept in your profile. Clear the year, or choose Prefer not to say for sex, on the Profile page to remove them.

With an account

You sign in with Google. Signing in uses only the basic openid, email and profile permissions. When you are signed in, each activity you analyse is saved to your library. We store:

  • Your account: the stable Google account identifier, your email address, your display name, and the profile picture address Google gives us. Your browser loads the picture directly from Google, and it is shown in the account menu.
  • Sign-in sessions: a one-way hash of your session token, your account, and when the session was created and expires. The token itself is kept only in your browser, in an HttpOnly cookie.
  • Profile settings: body mass, bike mass, your unit system, whether you keep original files, and your year of birth and sex if you give them.
  • Activity summaries: for each saved activity, its name, start time and sport, the name and SHA-256 hash of the file it came from, its distance, duration and elevation gain, and the analysis summary computed in your browser. The analysis summary includes best efforts, heart-rate figures, climb sections and data-quality flags. It does not include the per-second recording or route coordinates.
  • Original activity files: stored only if you turn on Keep original files in my library. A file is uploaded only while this setting is on. Turning it off deletes the stored originals and keeps the summaries.

Signed in, your settings are also kept in the local copy described under Without an account: your body mass, bike mass, unit preference, year of birth and sex, and the record that the first-analysis questions were answered on this device. That copy is updated when you change those settings, so signing out shows your latest values. It stays in your browser; the account keeps its own copy in your profile.

Google's own privacy policy governs what Google does with your account. We do not receive your Google password.

Where it is stored and who processes it

Account records, sessions, profile settings and activity summaries are stored in Cloudflare D1. Original files, when kept, are stored in a private Cloudflare R2 bucket. Summaries kept without an account are stored only in your browser's IndexedDB storage (see Without an account); we never receive them. Cloudflare Workers serves the application and its interface. Cloudflare processes these data as our infrastructure provider. Google processes your sign-in under its own policies. When backups are enabled, encrypted copies are stored as GitHub Actions artifacts (see below). We do not sell your data, and we do not give it to anyone other than these providers.

Cookies and similar technology

The app uses only strictly necessary cookies: a session cookie that keeps you signed in, and short-lived cookies (ten minutes) that protect the Google sign-in. We set no tracking or advertising cookies. Without an account, the app also uses your browser's IndexedDB and local storage, described under Without an account. They are not cookies, they stay in your browser, and they are not sent to us.

Logs and rate limits

Cloudflare handles each request, including the network address it came from, under its own policies. Cloudflare uses that address for rate limiting, which the application does not store. When the application hits an unexpected error, it logs a request identifier and the error type. Our code does not log request bodies, file contents, tokens or query strings. Cloudflare's request logs, kept for 3 days, record request URLs, including the single-use sign-in code returned by Google, which cannot be reused.

How long we keep it

  • Account, profile, activity summaries and original files stay until you delete them or delete your account.
  • Sessions end after 30 days without use, and in any case 90 days after they were created. Expired sessions are removed.
  • Cloudflare's request logs and the application's error logs are kept for three days.
  • Backups are not enabled at the date of this page. When they are, each backup is an encrypted copy of the database, kept for up to 90 days and then deleted. A backup also holds a list of the stored original-file objects (their keys and sizes), but not the files themselves. Deleted data can remain in backups until those backups expire.

How to delete your data

  • Turn off Keep original files in my library to delete all stored originals while keeping your summaries.
  • Delete an activity from the Saved library on your Profile page to remove its summary and any stored original file.
  • Without an account, choose Start over on your Profile page to remove the summaries kept in your browser.
  • Clear your year of birth or choose Prefer not to say for sex on the Profile page to remove them from your profile.
  • Choose Delete account on the Profile page. This removes your account, sessions, profile, all activity summaries and all stored original files. Deleted data can remain in encrypted backups, if backups are enabled, until those backups expire.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have rights to access, correct, delete or restrict the use of your personal data. To use them, open a privacy request as described above. Deleting your account in the app is always available without a request.

If you are signed in, you can download your saved data at any time. On the Profile page, choose Download my data. The file is one JSON document. It contains:

  • the export's format name and version, and when it was made;
  • your account name and email address;
  • your profile settings: body mass, bike mass, unit system, whether you keep original files, your year of birth and sex, and when the settings last changed;
  • for each saved activity: its library identifier, sport, start time, name, the name of the file it came from, whether an original file is stored, when it was saved, and its stored analysis summary, including the data-quality flags;
  • the number of saved activities that could not be read, which are therefore left out of the file;
  • notes on what the file leaves out and how values are written.

The file does not include original activity files.

Children

The service is not directed at children, and we do not knowingly collect their data.

Changes
